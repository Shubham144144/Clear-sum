What this calculator does

This tool works out what you actually keep from a sale after every real cost is subtracted: what you paid for the product, what it cost to ship, and what the platform takes in fees. It shows net profit per sale, your profit margin, and your ROI, then lets you test what happens if you raise your price.

How the calculation works

Most marketplaces charge a percentage-based fee on the selling price, and some add a small flat fee per order on top. Your net profit is what's left once product cost, shipping cost, and both fee types are subtracted from the selling price. Profit margin measures that profit against your selling price, while ROI measures it against what you actually spent up front — product and shipping — which is usually the more useful number for deciding whether a product is worth sourcing again.

The formula

The calculation is:

Net Profit = Selling Price − Product Cost − Shipping Cost − (Selling Price × Fee%) − Flat Fee
Profit Margin = Net Profit ÷ Selling Price × 100. ROI = Net Profit ÷ (Product Cost + Shipping Cost) × 100.

Worked example

Say you sell a product for $40 on Amazon, where it cost you $12 and $4 to ship. Amazon's referral fee runs about 15% of the price, or $6.00, with no flat fee. Subtracting $12 + $4 + $6.00 from $40 leaves a net profit of $18.00 — a profit margin of about 45% and an ROI of about 113% on the $16 you actually spent.

Practical use cases

  • Deciding whether a product idea is worth sourcing before you commit budget to it
  • Comparing the same product's real profit across Amazon, eBay, Etsy and your own Shopify store
  • Checking whether a price increase actually improves profit once fees scale with it
  • Spotting products where margin looks fine but ROI is too thin to justify the cash tied up

Advantages of checking profit before you list

Selling price and product cost alone can make a product look profitable when it isn't — platform fees quietly take a meaningful cut, and a flat fee per order can wipe out margin entirely on lower-priced items. Running the real numbers first means you find that out before you've bought inventory, not after.

Limitations to keep in mind

The platform fee presets are headline rates for a typical category and are meant as a starting point, not an exact quote — real fees vary by product category, account type, and fulfillment method (like Amazon FBA storage and fulfillment fees, which aren't modeled here). Currency conversion, sales tax or VAT, returns, and advertising spend also aren't included, so treat the result as a per-sale estimate rather than your full cost of doing business.

Common mistakes sellers make

  • Looking only at profit margin and ignoring ROI, which matters more when cash is limited
  • Forgetting that a flat fee per order hits low-priced items much harder than high-priced ones
  • Comparing platforms on fee percentage alone without checking flat fees and typical price points
  • Not re-checking margin after a price change, since fees that scale with price eat into part of the increase

Frequently asked questions

How is seller profit calculated?

Net profit is your selling price minus product cost, shipping cost, and platform fees — a percentage-based fee plus any flat fee per order.

What's the difference between profit margin and ROI?

Margin is profit as a share of your selling price. ROI is profit as a share of what you actually spent, which shows how hard your invested money is working.

Why do platform fees vary so much between marketplaces?

Amazon and eBay charge for access to their built-in customer base, Etsy combines a transaction fee with payment processing, and Shopify only charges payment processing since you bring your own traffic.

Are the platform fee presets exact?

No — they're typical headline rates used as a starting point. Every field is editable so you can match your actual category rate.

A few practical tips

  • Check ROI, not just margin, when deciding between two products with limited budget
  • Re-run the numbers per platform before choosing where to list a given product
  • Build in a buffer for returns and ad spend, since neither is included in this estimate

Conclusion

A selling price only tells part of the story — what you actually keep depends on product cost, shipping, and how much the platform takes. Run a product through this calculator before you commit budget to it, and compare it across platforms before deciding where to list.
